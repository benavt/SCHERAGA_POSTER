Proteins function through conformational ensembles. To assess the ability of current foundational structure inference models to predict multiple protein states, we generated 22,000 monomer models (11,000 per target; 1,000 per sampling method per target) for E. coli Adk (PDB IDs 4AKE/1AKE)¹,² and Mad2 mitotic spindle assembly checkpoint protein (PDB IDs 1DUJ/1S2H)³,⁴ using 11 different Artificial Intelligence Sampling (AIS) methods. Four AIS methods involve applying a stochastic 40% MSA column masking to foundational structure inference models, adapted from Kalakoti and Wallner (Fig. 1a).⁵ Shared Cα pair distances from the AIS models were used as features for clustering with UMAP, PCA, t-SNE and Bonsai. These AIS procedures produced models with best TM-scores (US-align) of 0.97/0.99 against open/closed adenylate kinase references (4AKE/1AKE) and 0.80/0.83 against open/closed Mad2 references (1DUJ/1S2H). Models were used as templates with Boltz2 in no-MSA mode to retrieve reported pTM scores, similar to the AFRank methodology proposed by Roney and Ovchinnikov (Fig. 1b).⁶ Each target was analyzed on four A100 GPUs in approximately 24 hours. This AIS protocol is continued on a set of 500+ protein targets with experimental evidence of multiple states.
